3 Red Flag Detector – Northline Athletics

Unethical behavior can happen at any level of a business, and incentive structures often push people toward it. Six things were said inside Northline Athletics last quarter. Mark each one as a red flag or not. Each correct flag is worth 1 point (6 total).

Types: Falsifying Information · Concealing Data · Misusing Company Property · Incentive-Driven Shortcut

# What was said or done Red Flag? Type
1 A regional manager rounds 4,900 units sold up to 5,000 on the report that triggers her bonus at 5,000.
2 A designer takes home a sample bottle from the prototype shelf to show her family.
3 A lab tech files the failed stress test in a folder nobody opens, and reports only the passing tests upward.
4 A supervisor tells the team that anyone who misses the shipping deadline will be moved off the account.
5 The head of quality control uses the company van every weekend to run a side business.
6 A sales rep tells a buyer the bottles are dishwasher safe. Nobody has ever tested that.

Look at #1 and #4 together. Explain how an incentive structure can produce unethical behavior even when nobody involved thinks of themselves as dishonest. (2 pts keyword-based)

Model Answer: The incentive structure at Northline Athletics directly rewards meeting a specific sales threshold (5,000 units) and punishes missing a shipping deadline. When a bonus or job security depends on hitting a number, individuals may rationalize small dishonest actions—like rounding up 4,900 to 5,000 or cutting corners—as necessary to meet the target. Neither person set out to be dishonest, but the system made the unethical option the rational one. The company designed the incentive, so it owns part of the outcome.

SoleSwap is a sneaker resale marketplace that connects independent sellers with buyers. SoleSwap's stated core value is transparency. Recently, the platform discovered that some sellers have been listing used sneakers as "new" and inflating the original retail price to make their profit margins look smaller than they are. SoleSwap's data team estimates that roughly 15% of listings contain at least one misleading claim.

SoleSwap is considering two responses:

  • Option A: Send all sellers a reminder email about the platform's honesty policy and update the listing guidelines page.
  • Option B: Launch an active verification system in which SoleSwap staff review flagged listings, remove those with false claims, and suspend sellers who repeatedly violate the policy. Sellers who pass verification receive a "Verified Accurate" badge on their listings.

a. Using the data provided and the concept of ethical behavior, compare Options A and B against SoleSwap's stated core value of transparency and its goal of maintaining buyer trust. Then recommend which option SoleSwap should adopt. Justify your recommendation. (5 pts keyword-based)

Model Answer: Option A is weakly aligned with transparency because a reminder email does not address the 15% of listings already containing misleading claims or enforce consequences for dishonest sellers. Option B is strongly aligned with transparency because active verification directly removes false listings and creates a visible, credible signal (the badge) that listings are accurate. Option B better maintains buyer trust because buyers receive confirmed, accurate information before purchasing, whereas Option A leaves misleading listings in place. However, Option B has higher operational costs and places a burden on sellers. Despite this trade-off, SoleSwap should adopt Option B because it directly supports the core value of transparency and the goal of maintaining buyer trust, using evidence from the scenario.

AerialEye is a commercial drone-photography service that captures images for real estate listings, construction sites, and outdoor events. AerialEye's stated core value is client trust. The company recently surveyed 120 current and former clients to evaluate their experiences.

Survey Findings (120 respondents):

  • 68% of respondents said they chose AerialEye specifically because of its reputation for honest, accurate project estimates.
  • 41% reported that a pilot had delivered final images that did not match the scope agreed upon in the project contract, without notifying the client in advance.
  • 29% said the undisclosed changes caused them to miss a listing deadline or project milestone.
  • 52% of dissatisfied clients said they would not hire AerialEye again and would warn others.
  • 0 of the affected clients had received a proactive explanation or apology from AerialEye before the survey was conducted.

AerialEye's leadership is reviewing this data to decide how to respond.

(A) Identify the type of unethical behavior reflected in the survey data and explain how it relates to AerialEye's core value. (2 pts keyword-based)

Model Answer: The unethical behavior is concealing or failing to disclose information—specifically, pilots delivered images outside the agreed scope without notifying clients in advance. This conflicts with AerialEye's core value of client trust because concealing project changes from clients undermines the honest, transparent relationship that trust requires.

(B) Explain how this unethical behavior could affect AerialEye's profitability. Use at least one specific finding from the survey data in your explanation. (2 pts keyword-based)

Model Answer: The unethical behavior could reduce profitability because 52% of dissatisfied clients said they would not rehire AerialEye and would warn others. This means lost repeat business and negative word-of-mouth that reduces new client acquisition, which lowers revenue. Additionally, 41% experienced undisclosed changes, which could lead to refunds, lawsuits, or reputational damage that further reduces profitability.

(C) Recommend one specific mechanism AerialEye's leadership could use to encourage ethical behavior going forward. Explain how that mechanism would address the behavior identified in part (A). (2 pts keyword-based)

Model Answer: AerialEye should implement a code of conduct that explicitly requires pilots to notify clients of any scope change before delivering final images. This mechanism directly addresses the behavior identified in part (A) by making the expected behavior clear and enforceable, and by ensuring that clients are informed of any deviations from the contract before they receive the final product.

6 Tech Company Dilemma – FRQ Practice

Scenario: A tech company discovers a minor bug in their new software that could potentially lead to a small data leak for 1% of users. Fixing the bug will delay the product launch by two months, costing the company $5 million in lost sales and potentially causing the stock price to drop.

1. Identify the Stakeholders: Who is affected by this decision? (2 pts keyword-based)

Model Answer: Customers (potential data leak), Shareholders (stock price drop), Employees (job security if sales drop), and the Company's Management (reputation).

2. The Trade-off: What is the short-term gain vs. the long-term risk? (2 pts keyword-based)

Model Answer: The short-term gain is $5 million in sales and maintaining the stock price. The long-term risk is a massive loss of customer trust, potential lawsuits, and a damaged brand reputation if the data leak occurs.

3. What should the company do? Justify your decision using ethical principles. (3 pts keyword-based)

Model Answer: The company should fix the bug and delay the launch. While it costs $5 million now, the long-term cost of a data breach—both financially and reputationally—is much higher. Ethical principles like transparency and accountability require protecting customers' data above short-term profit.
